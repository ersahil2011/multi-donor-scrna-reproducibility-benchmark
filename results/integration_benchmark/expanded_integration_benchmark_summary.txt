EXPANDED INTEGRATION BENCHMARK SUMMARY
======================================

Benchmark design
----------------
Four workflows were evaluated on the identical  donor-balanced subset of 6,955 cells.
All workflows used 2,000 HVGs, 20 PCs,  neighbour k = 20, resolution = 0.6, and seed 2026.

Interpretation
--------------
All integrated workflows improved donor mixing relative  to unintegrated PCA while retaining high graph connectivity.
RPCA produced the highest scaled median iLISI (0.4490),  the lowest kBET rejection rate among integrated workflows  (0.8687), and the highest NMI agreement (0.7231).
Harmony produced the highest ARI agreement (0.5871),  the highest graph connectivity (0.9909), and the shortest  runtime among integrated workflows (10.5 seconds).
CCA produced the highest biological silhouette value  (0.4194) but required the longest runtime (82.3 seconds).
No method dominated every metric. RPCA was retained as the  primary workflow because it provided the strongest overall  balance between donor mixing, clustering agreement, biological  structure preservation, and computational cost.

Interpretive limitations
------------------------
ARI and NMI quantify agreement with the retained full-data  RPCA partition and must not be interpreted as accuracy against  independent biological ground truth.
The high kBET rejection rates indicate that residual donor  structure remains after integration and should be reported  rather than concealed.
These findings apply to the five healthy synovial donors  evaluated here and do not establish universal superiority  across tissues, diseases, platforms, or populations.
